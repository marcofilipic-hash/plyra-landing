# PLYRA — Notes: custom hardware + Interview step clarification

Two additions to carry into the next round of work on the landing (Antigravity).

## 1. Custom-designed hardware (tiradores / pulls)

PLYRA will design their own drawer pulls and hardware in-house, not just source third-party hardware. This becomes another differentiator to offer alongside the cabinet fronts/finishes.

Implications for the site:
- This could eventually get its own mention somewhere in the page — either as a line in the Proposal statement, a bullet in the Process/materials area, or (later, once there's photography of actual hardware) its own small section or addition to the "detail + context" material pairing (see `PLYRA_reform_structure_brief.md`) — a close-up of a custom pull could sit alongside the cabinet edge/canto detail as another "made by hand, made by us" moment.
- No copy or layout decided yet — just noting the fact so it's not lost, and so it's considered next time the Process/materials sections get revisited.

## 2. Clarifying what the "Interview" step in Process actually means

The first step of the 4-step Process (Interview → Design → Fabrication → Delivery) isn't just an intake meeting — its real purpose is to understand how the client actually lives and organizes their things, so the kitchen gets designed around those real habits rather than a generic layout.

This is worth reflecting in the Process section's copy for that step (currently likely a placeholder or generic description) — something that communicates "we're not just taking measurements, we're learning how you cook, store, and move through the space" rather than a bureaucratic-sounding "initial consultation."

Also worth cross-referencing with the 10-step qualification form (`PLYRA_contact_form_brief.md`) — questions like "qué es lo más importante para ellos (materiales, color, funcionalidad, precio)" and the open question "contanos tu proyecto" are effectively the first layer of that same listening process, so the form and the Interview step in Process should probably feel like one continuous idea rather than two disconnected things when the copy gets written.
