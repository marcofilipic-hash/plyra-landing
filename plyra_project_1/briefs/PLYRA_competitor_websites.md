# PLYRA — Website Comparison: Furniture & Kitchen Brands

Research pass on brands with a similar strategy to PLYRA (design-forward, small-batch or made-to-order furniture/kitchens), to compare website approaches before the next design pass.

## Reform (reformcph.com) — closest direct comparison

Reform is a Danish kitchen brand that redesigns cabinet fronts (originally for IKEA carcasses, now with their own systems too) using well-known architects and designers. This is the single closest comp to PLYRA in category and business model.

- Hero: no dramatic single image — leads with an aspirational tagline ("Let's bring your vision to life") and positions itself as a design partner, not just a product.
- Collections are named and credited to specific designers/architects — this lends prestige and gives visitors a "line" to choose, similar to PLYRA's collections (orange/graphite/petrol).
- Pricing is deliberately absent from the homepage. Instead of a number, they offer a "Get a price estimate" tool and a sketch/planning tool called OUTLINE — a consultative funnel rather than transparent pricing.
- Multiple CTAs at different commitment levels: "Get price estimate," "Book a meeting," "Order samples," "Visit showroom" — a multi-touch funnel rather than one single CTA.
- Photography favors showroom/retail imagery over lifestyle shots, which grounds the brand in a tangible, physical experience.
- Navigation splits Kitchens / Wardrobe / Discover (editorial content) / Planning (tools) — serving both browsers and serious buyers from the same nav.

**Relevant to PLYRA:** this is the brand to watch most closely, since it proves a design-forward, one-thing-well kitchen studio can scale a real business on a similar footing. The price-estimate-tool instead of a fixed number is worth considering for later (though your explicit brief so far has wanted a visible reference price + direct CTA, which is a valid alternate strategy to Reform's "estimate on request").

## FRAMA (framacph.com) — Copenhagen furniture & home goods

Broader furniture/lifestyle brand, not kitchen-specific, but same design register (minimal, editorial, "honest materials").

- Clean, hierarchical layout: sticky header, hero imagery, bestsellers carousel, lifestyle/editorial content blocks ("styling guide," "seeking repose").
- Prices ARE shown directly on product cards, in local currency — no friction, no "request a quote." This is the opposite approach from Reform.
- Tone is understated and aspirational, not salesy — no discount banners, no urgency language.
- Embedded video content showing furniture "in motion," global shipping selector, and a wholesale/professional section (B2B).

**Relevant to PLYRA:** shows that direct pricing and an editorial, non-pushy tone aren't mutually exclusive — FRAMA does both. Useful counter-example to Reform's price-on-request model.

## TAKT (taktcph.com) — already reviewed earlier in this project

Pure editorial/storytelling format (an interview-style article, "TAKT Moves In"), no pricing or purchase CTAs anywhere, full-width photography carrying most of the weight. Works because TAKT is an established brand that doesn't need to convert on this particular page — it's brand-building content, not a sales landing page. Noted previously as a tone/photography reference, not a structural one, since PLYRA's brief explicitly needs the page to convert (price + CTA visible).

## Props Furniture (Awwwards SOTD) — e-commerce furniture, design-and-motion reference

Different category (direct e-commerce, not made-to-order/bespoke), but relevant as a pure "very well designed furniture website" reference:

- Warm, restrained palette (beige/deep brown), strong preloader, parallax and gesture-based interactions.
- Shop / Lookbook / About structure, with a dedicated lookbook section for pure visual storytelling separate from the shop.
- Scored well on design (7.58/10 Awwwards jury) but flagged for accessibility (6.8/10) — a reminder that heavy animation/motion can trade off against usability if not handled carefully.

**Relevant to PLYRA:** good reference for interaction polish and a "lookbook" style section, less relevant for pricing/CTA strategy since it's a straightforward e-commerce checkout flow, not a lead-gen/consultation flow like PLYRA needs.

## Takeaways for PLYRA

1. The two live "operating models" among direct comps are: **fixed reference price + direct CTA** (closer to FRAMA's transparency, and to what you already chose for the hero) vs. **price-on-request + consultative funnel** (Reform's model, multiple CTAs at different commitment levels). You've already committed to the first — worth knowing Reform, the closest kitchen-specific comp, does the opposite, and revisit this only if conversion data later suggests otherwise.
2. Designer/architect credits on collections (Reform) is a credibility device PLYRA could borrow once real collaborators are attached to collections — noted as a future idea, not urgent now.
3. A short "lookbook" page/section, separate from the sales-oriented sections, is a pattern several of these use to let photography do pure brand-building work without pressure to convert on that page.
